# CivicLedger Pitch Deck

## Slide 1: CivicLedger

SDG 4 · SDG 9 · Community · Safety

Verified service-learning and safer field activities for schools and communities.

*Hackathon proposal · Synthetic data only · Supplementary record, not an official one*

## Slide 2: Service is valuable, but today’s records fall short

- **Learning is invisible:** Hour logs show time served, not the skills students gained.
- **Field safety runs on paper:** Sign-in sheets are the main way to know who is on site, and incidents are reported late.
- **Records are hard to verify:** Logs and certificates can be edited without a trace, and checking one means calling the school.

## Slide 3: One record from published event to verified credential

| Pillar | In the MVP | Next, in the pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Community | Service events for assigned sections, with one-time registration. | Events link to a documented community need. |
| Education | Approved hours and supplementary certificates. SDG 4. | Skill tags, reflections and credentials that list skills. |
| Safety | Check-in and check-out with a live roster. | Briefing, emergency contacts, incident flag. |
| Trust | Open verification of approved records, no wallet needed. SDG 9. | — |

## Slide 4: How it works, in six steps

1. **Publish:** Coordinator publishes an event.
2. **Register:** Eligible students sign up once.
3. **Scan:** Rotating QR check-in and check-out with a live roster.
4. **Approve:** Coordinator reviews exceptions and approves hours.
5. **Anchor:** A private fingerprint is stored on a test blockchain.
6. **Verify:** Anyone scans the certificate QR and sees one clear result.

## Slide 5: What the blockchain proves, and what it does not

### It proves

- An approved record has not been changed since approval
- A certificate is current, revoked or superseded
- Anyone can check it without calling the school

### It does not prove

- That a student physically took part
- That the school officially recognizes the certificate

Participation evidence comes from rotating QR codes, server time, registration checks and coordinator review.

*No names, emails or student numbers are ever placed on the blockchain*

## Slide 6: Aligned with SDG 4 and SDG 9

### SDG 4 · Quality Education

- **4.4** Skills for employment and civic life: verifiable service hours and certificates now; skill tags and skills-based certificates in the pilot.
- **4.7** Sustainable development and global citizenship: service events recorded from registration to approval now; events tied to a community need in the pilot.

### SDG 9 · Industry, Innovation, Infrastructure

- **9.c** Access to ICT: a mobile web app with no wallet and no install.
- **9.5** Technological capability: blockchain as shared verification infrastructure for schools.

*Closest targets per feature. Impact is measured in a pilot, not claimed here.*

## Slide 7: The live demo, in four minutes

| Step | What the audience sees | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coordinator publishes an event | Scoped event publishing |
| 2 | Students register and scan the rotating QR | Safe attendance, live roster |
| 3 | Coordinator approves hours; proof moves to Confirmed | Approval, then blockchain proof |
| 4 | Signer issues a certificate | Verifiable credential |
| 5 | A verifier scans the certificate QR: Verified | Open verification |
| 6 | A stored value is altered: Mismatch; then revoke: Revoked | Tamper detection and revocation |

*Synthetic data. A preconfirmed record is ready if the test network is slow.*

## Slide 8: What a pilot would measure

- **Education:** Share of approved records with a skill tag and a reflection.
- **Community:** Events linked to a documented need, and partners served.
- **Safety:** Time to confirm who is on site, and time to flag an incident.
- **Trust:** Outside verification checks, and altered records caught.

No baseline exists yet. The pilot measures first, then the school sets targets with the team. Skill tags, community needs and incident flags are built for the pilot.

## Slide 9: Six modules, built in two days

### Day 1

- Accounts, roles and scope
- Events and registration
- QR attendance and live roster
- Approval, versioning, audit

### Day 2

- Blockchain proof pipeline
- Certificates and public verification
- Dashboard and reports
- Polish, tests and rehearsal

Feature freeze at midday on day 2. Community needs, skills credentials and the safety layer follow in the pilot, after scope approval. Partner portal, donations, project tracking and notifications stay out of scope.

## Slide 10: Service recorded. Impact trusted.

To move from demo to pilot, we need four decisions from the school:

- One section and one community partner to pilot with
- Rules for hour rounding, certificate threshold and who signs
- A data-privacy review before any real student data is used
- Approval for the community, skills and safety features

*Certificates are supplementary records. Integrity checks do not prove participation.*
